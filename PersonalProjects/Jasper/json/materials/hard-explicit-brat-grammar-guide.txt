CURRENT PROJECT RULE:

    Jasper is an adult.
    Jasper uses they/she pronouns.

FIRST-PERSON NARRATION SET:

    I        = subject
    me       = object
    my       = possessive determiner
    mine     = possessive pronoun
    myself   = reflexive/intensive

Examples:

    I folded my arms.
    Iroh looked at me.
    The cup in front of me was mine.
    I reminded myself not to grin.

The narrator should NOT become:

    Jasper folded their arms.          [wrong POV: third person]
    You folded your arms.               [wrong POV: second-person narration]
    She folded her arms.                [wrong POV: third-person narration]

IMPORTANT DIRECT-DIALOGUE EXCEPTION:

Other characters speaking TO Jasper will naturally use "you" and "your." This does NOT break first-person POV.

Correct:

    I crossed my arms.
    Iroh smiled. "Jasper, you are trying very hard not to laugh at me."

Correct:

    "You know exactly what you're doing," Snape said.
    I absolutely did.

Incorrect:

    You crossed your arms while Iroh smiled at you.

The prohibition is against second-person NARRATION, not against ordinary direct address in dialogue.

======================================================================

William requested an R-rated private project with no minors allowed.

Important terminology note:

The Motion Picture Association's official R rating does NOT mean adults only. Under the U.S. film rating system, people under 17 may attend an R-rated film with an accompanying parent/adult guardian. The official adults-only film category is NC-17.

Therefore the program should NOT rely on "R" as its age control.

Recommended project label:

    rating_display: "18+ Private / Hard Explicit — No Fade to Black"
    audience: "Adults Only"
    minimum_age: 18
    minors_allowed: false

This revision intentionally goes beyond ordinary movie-rating shorthand. The actual project rule is unambiguous: 18+ only, explicit adult content may be shown on-page, and fade-to-black is not an allowed substitute when an explicit scene has been selected.

If the program also uses AO3-style terminology internally, consider separate fields:

    romance_rating: Mature or Explicit
    project_display_rating: 18+ Private / Hard Explicit — No Fade to Black

Do not let rating vocabulary substitute for actual age validation.

4A. HARD-EXPLICIT OUTPUT CONTRACT — NO FADE, NO CENSORSHIP DEVICES
======================================================================

This section OVERRIDES any older language in the source JSON or earlier guides that says:

    fade to black
    implied intimacy only
    hidden nudity
    tasteful cutaway

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4A.1 REQUIRED EXPLICITNESS MODE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommended project defaults:

    adult_content.enabled = true
    explicitness_mode = "hard_explicit"
    fade_to_black_allowed = false
    off_page_substitution_allowed = false
    hidden_nudity_censorship = false
    explicit_anatomical_language_required = true
    character_specific_dirty_talk = true
    aftercare_required = true

The rule is not "every chapter must contain sex."

The rule is:

    IF the chapter chooses an adult sexual scene,
    THEN the scene must remain on-page rather than evading its own content.

A chapter may still be entirely romantic, domestic, funny, plot-heavy, or nonsexual. The hard-explicit requirement activates when the chapter itself crosses into sexual intimacy.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4A.2 FADE-TO-BLACK FAILURE CONDITIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The generator should flag a scene as FAILED HARD-EXPLICIT OUTPUT when it uses a transition whose purpose is to skip the sexual scene after beginning it.

Common censorship/fade patterns to flag in sexual context include:

    "one thing led to another"
    "the rest was a blur"
    "we lost ourselves in each other" followed by a time jump
    "later..." used to skip the encounter
    "the night passed in passion"
    "what happened next was private"
    "I woke the next morning" immediately after sexual escalation
    scene break / chapter break placed specifically to avoid adult content
    ellipsis used as a substitute for the scene

These phrases are NOT globally forbidden English. They are forbidden when they function as a censorship device that replaces the explicit scene the generator was asked to write.

A hard-explicit scene should proceed through its actual dramatic arc on-page and then reconnect to the relationship afterward.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4A.3 NO HIDDEN NUDITY / NO STRATEGIC CENSORSHIP
---------------------------------------------------------------------

When a character is nude, the prose may say that they are nude and may plainly name visible adult anatomy when relevant to Jasper's viewpoint.

The generator should NOT invent censorship devices solely to hide the body, such as:

    sheets that inexplicably cover exactly the genitals at all times;
    hair positioned like a censorship bar;

    darkness/shadow introduced only to avoid describing nudity;
    a camera-like cut above the waist after clothes come off;
    sudden vagueness such as "everything below" or "private areas" when direct language fits.

Important realism rule:

    Do not force visibility that Jasper's physical viewpoint could not actually have.

"No hidden nudity" means DO NOT CREATE artificial obstructions for censorship. It does not mean narrating impossible sight lines.

5. PRONOUN + GRAMMAR RULES FOR THE GENERATOR
======================================================================

This section should be treated almost like a parser/linter specification.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 FIRST PERSON: I / ME / MY / MINE / MYSELF
---------------------------------------------------------------------

I = subject form.

    I laughed.
    I opened the door.
    I wanted to argue.

ME = object form.

    Iroh watched me.
    Greg sat beside me.
    Snape handed the book to me.

MY = possessive determiner; it comes before a noun.

    my hands
    my coat
    my breath
    my choice

MINE = possessive pronoun; it replaces the noun phrase.

    The mug was mine.
    The decision was mine.

MYSELF = reflexive or intensive form.

    I steadied myself.
    I did it myself.

Do NOT use "myself" merely because the sentence sounds formal.

Wrong:

    Iroh handed the cup to myself.

Correct:

    Iroh handed the cup to me.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 JASPER'S THIRD-PERSON PRONOUN SETS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

When another character talks ABOUT Jasper, or when a third-person data field needs pronouns, use the configured they/she sets.

THEY SET:

    subject: they
    object: them
    possessive determiner: their
    possessive pronoun: theirs
    reflexive: themself OR themselves

For this project, "themself" is recommended for singular consistency, but "themselves" is also widely accepted. Pick ONE project default and lint for consistency.

Verb agreement stays grammatically plural in form even when "they" refers to one person:

    they are
    they were
    they have
    they do
    they want

NOT:

    they is
    they has
    they does

SHE SET:

    subject: she
    object: her
    possessive determiner: her
    possessive pronoun: hers
    reflexive: herself

Verb agreement:

    she is
    she was
    she has
    she does
    she wants

NAME AGREEMENT:

    Jasper is
    Jasper was
    Jasper has
    Jasper does
    Jasper wants

Research basis:
- MLA Style Center, "How do I use singular they?": https://style.mla.org/using-singular-they/
- MLA Style Center, pronoun referents: https://style.mla.org/pronouns-and-referents/
- Cambridge Grammar, personal pronouns: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/pronouns-personal-i-me-you-he-it-they-etc

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3 MIXED THEY/SHE SHOULD NOT BE RANDOM WORD SALAD
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Jasper uses they/she. A generator can technically switch every sentence, but doing so mechanically often produces confusing antecedents and makes the prose feel synthetic.

Recommended method:

- Both sets are always valid.
- Do not force alternation.
- Prefer one pronoun chain within a sentence or short paragraph.
- Switch naturally across paragraph/scene boundaries.
- Use "Jasper" to reset the antecedent before switching when clarity would benefit.
- Never switch because one pronoun is treated as "more correct."
- Never make she/her automatically mean a more feminine mood or they/them automatically mean a more neutral mood.

Clear:

    Zuko glanced toward the counter. "Jasper said they would close tonight."
    Iroh nodded. "She has done enough for one day."

Even clearer when there are several characters:

    Zuko glanced toward the counter. "Jasper said they would close tonight."
    Iroh watched me stack the last cup. "Jasper has done enough for one day. She should rest."

Potentially confusing:

    Katara told Toph that Jasper said she would bring their coat because they were cold.

The generator should rewrite ambiguous chains rather than blindly preserving pronoun variety.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.4 DIRECT ADDRESS "YOU" IS ALLOWED IN DIALOGUE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

A first-person story can contain "you" in dialogue without becoming second-person narration.

Correct:

    "Jasper, you are impossible," he said.
    I smiled. "You like me anyway."

Correct:

    "Your tea is getting cold."
    "Then stop distracting me."

The lint rule should therefore be:

    ban second-person NARRATIVE viewpoint

not:

    ban the token "you"

A crude global replacement of "you" would destroy dialogue.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.5 POV LEAKAGE LINTER
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Flag likely errors when narration outside quotation marks contains patterns such as:

    you walked
    you looked
    you felt
    your hands
    your breath

unless they are:
- internal quoted self-address;
- a letter/message addressed to someone;
- quoted dialogue;
- intentionally embedded second-person text.

Also flag third-person self-reference in narration:

    Jasper walked
    they walked
    she walked

when the grammatical referent is the narrator rather than another Jasper in quoted dialogue.

The expected narrative self-reference is always I/me/my/mine/myself.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6 GRAMMAR IS A HARD VALIDATION LAYER, NOT A STYLE SUGGESTION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

For this project, grammar and POV correctness should be validated BEFORE prose is accepted as a finished chapter.

Priority order:

    1. POV/person correctness
    2. pronoun/reference correctness
    3. subject-verb agreement
    4. possessive/reflexive correctness
    5. tense consistency
    6. dialogue punctuation and direct address
    7. character voice
    8. stylistic variation

If a stylish sentence violates the POV model, fix the sentence.
Do not keep a beautiful line that accidentally turns Jasper into third person.

5.9 BODY-OWNERSHIP PRONOUN RULE — CRITICAL FOR ADULT SCENES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Because Jasper narrates in first person, Jasper's own body uses FIRST-PERSON possession in narration.

Correct narrative ownership:

    my hand
    my mouth
    my thigh
    my clit
    my vulva
    my breath
    my body

Wrong in Jasper's narration when referring to Jasper's body:

    your clit
    their clit
    her clit
    Jasper's clit

Those forms can be correct in OTHER grammatical contexts:

A character speaking TO Jasper:

    "your ..."

A character speaking ABOUT Jasper to someone else:

    "their ..." or "her ..."

A third-person metadata field:

    Jasper's ...

This ownership rule should be one of the strongest automated checks because erotic generators commonly leak from first person into second person specifically around body-part phrases.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.10 DIRECT ADDRESS COMMAS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Use commas around a person's name/title when it is direct address.

Correct:

    "Jasper, come here."
    "You know what you're doing, Jasper."
    "My dear, that was a very obvious loophole."

Incorrect:

    "Jasper come here."

Do not confuse a direct-address noun with the grammatical subject of the sentence.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.11 DIALOGUE TAG PUNCTUATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Dialogue tag:

    "I know," I said.
    "Do you?" he asked.

Action beat:

    "I know." I folded my arms.

Do not write:

    "I know." I said.

when "said" is functioning as a dialogue tag.

After dialogue ending in ? or !, the following dialogue tag remains lowercase unless it begins with a proper noun:

    "Really?" he asked.
    "Stop that!" Greg said, laughing.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.12 QUOTE-AWARE 'YOU' DETECTION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The generator MUST NOT ban the word "you."

It must distinguish:

A. DIALOGUE / DIRECT ADDRESS — valid:

    "You are impossible," he said.

B. NARRATION — invalid if Jasper suddenly becomes second person:

    You are impossible, and you know it.

C. INTERNAL SELF-ADDRESS — valid if intentionally quoted or marked as thought:

    Come on, Jasper. You can do this.

D. LETTER / MESSAGE / SONG LYRIC / QUOTED TEXT — may be valid depending on context.

Implementation advice:

    tokenize quotation spans first;
    do not run the same second-person rule blindly across dialogue and narration;
    inspect narrative clauses for subject "you" referring to Jasper.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.13 FIRST-PERSON KNOWLEDGE LIMIT
---------------------------------------------------------------------

First person is not only a pronoun system. It is an INFORMATION LIMIT.

Jasper may narrate:

- what I see;
- what I hear;
- what I remember;
- what I feel;
- what I infer;
- what another character tells me;
- what I believe another character's expression means.

Jasper may NOT state another character's unspoken thoughts as objective fact unless the setting provides an actual mind-reading mechanism.

Wrong locked-POV prose:

    He wondered whether I would kiss him.

Better:

    His gaze dropped to my mouth, and I had a pretty good guess what he was wondering.

This matters in romance because mind-reading prose quietly turns first person into omniscient third person even when every pronoun still looks correct.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.14 TENSE LOCK
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Choose a narrative tense at series level:

    first_person_past
or
    first_person_present

Do not drift between them accidentally.

Past:

    I folded my arms. He smiled.

Present:

    I fold my arms. He smiles.

Dialogue can naturally use any tense required by meaning.
The restriction applies to narrative frame, not every verb in the story.

Recommended default for longfic:

    narrative_tense = first_person_past

because the current guide/examples predominantly read naturally in past-tense narrative.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.15 PRONOUN-CHAIN STABILITY FOR THEY/SHE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Mixed pronouns should feel human, not randomized.

Hard rules:

1. Never alternate solely because a counter says it is time to switch.
2. Keep one external pronoun set through a short referential chain when switching would cause ambiguity.
3. Switch after a clear noun reset ("Jasper") when multiple people share she/her or they/them.
4. Do not encode mood, dominance, femininity, softness, anger, or sexual intensity into pronoun choice.
5. Do not make sexual content automatically switch Jasper to she/her.
6. Do not make nonsexual content automatically switch Jasper to they/them.

Suggested deterministic modes:

    mode A: scene_weighted_mixed
    mode B: paragraph_stable_mixed
    mode C: character_speaker_preference_if_configured

For maximum grammatical clarity, paragraph_stable_mixed is the safest default.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.16 AMBIGUOUS ANTECEDENT REWRITE RULE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

If a sentence contains multiple possible referents for "she," "her," "they," "them," "their," or "hers," REWRITE IT.

Do not ask the reader to decode the pronoun chain.

Weak:

    Katara told Jasper she should take her coat because she was cold.

Possible rewrite:

    Katara handed me my coat. "Put this on. You're freezing."

In first-person fic, many ambiguous third-person chains can be eliminated by returning Jasper to I/me/my.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5.17 PREPOSITION + OBJECT FORM
---------------------------------------------------------------------

After an ordinary preposition, use object form:

    to me
    for me
    with me
    beside me
    between him and me

Not:

    to I
    for I
    between him and I

Reflexive is not a fancy object pronoun:

    He handed it to me.

not:

    He handed it to myself.

Cambridge's reflexive guidance supports using reflexives when subject and object refer to the same person or for emphasis, not as a general formal replacement for object pronouns.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

my + noun:

11. CONSENT ARCHITECTURE FOR THE GENERATOR
======================================================================

The BDSM research base consistently treats consent as central to distinguishing consensual power exchange from abuse.

A useful conceptual model is the "4Cs" proposed in BDSM literature:

    Caring
    Communication
    Consent
    Caution

The project does not have to display this acronym to Jasper, but it is a good internal writing model.

Research:
- Williams, Thomas, Prior & Christensen, "From SSC and RACK to the 4Cs," 2014.
  Accessible abstract/summary: https://go.gale.com/ps/i.do?asid=9ae6e2ae&id=GALE%7CA419762806&it=r&p=AONE&u=googlescholar&v=2.1
- NCSF Consent Counts materials: https://ncsfreedom.org/2download-consent-signs/
- Dunkley & Brotto, 2020: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31010393/

---------------------------------------------------------------------
11.1 CONSENT DOES NOT HAVE TO SOUND LIKE A CONTRACT
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Good fiction can communicate consent through:

- direct questions;
- familiar negotiated rituals;
- checking Jasper's face/body language AND receiving affirmative participation;
- Jasper actively pulling the character closer;
- Jasper asking for something;
- a teasing confirmation;
- remembered boundaries;
- pausing when uncertainty appears;
- immediate response to a stop signal.

For an established dynamic, every scene does not need to repeat a legalistic checklist, but the story should make it clear that both people know the game they are playing.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
11.2 "BRAT" IS NOT A CONSENT BYPASS TOKEN
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Never generate logic like:

    Jasper is a brat -> resistance = consent.

Instead:

    Jasper is a brat -> certain pre-negotiated forms of playful resistance MAY be part of the erotic script.

Current consent still governs.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
11.3 INTOXICATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Because BDSM research finds particularly strong concern around capacity and explicit negotiation, adult-mode scenes should default to sober consent for new or intense kink scenes.

If alcohol/drugs are present in the story:
- do not introduce a new BDSM limit negotiation while impaired;
- do not treat intoxication as automatic permission;
- established characters may have previously negotiated boundaries, but the generator should still avoid portraying impaired ambiguity as romantic.
